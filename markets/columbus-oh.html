The Market

Columbus Industrial Market: Q2 2026 Snapshot

Metro-wide figures from named public and brokerage sources, as of Q2 2026. Updated quarterly.

Greater Columbus industrial vacancy fell to 4.7% in Q2 2026, the seventh straight quarterly decline and the lowest level since 2023.[1] The market absorbed 3.4 million SF in the quarter. Average net asking rent was $6.33 per SF, down 1.4% from a year earlier.[1]

Those figures are dominated by large distribution buildings, and no published report breaks out small-bay space. Cushman & Wakefield’s building-type split is the closest view: office-service space, its category nearest to flex, asked $10.42 net per SF at 6.9% vacancy, against $6.33 across all categories.[1] That is a building-type classification, not a size cut.

Columbus reaches 47% of the U.S. population and 33% of Canada’s within a one-day truck drive, according to the Columbus Regional Airport Authority, which oversees Foreign-Trade Zone 138.[2] Franklin County unemployment was 3.4% in August 2026 (not seasonally adjusted, preliminary).[3] The Columbus metro counts about 1.2 million nonfarm jobs, including about 77,000 in manufacturing and about 226,000 in trade, transportation and utilities.[4] Of Franklin County’s 695,916 employed residents, 52,228 work in manufacturing and 52,709 in transportation, warehousing and utilities (2019–2023 survey estimates).[5]

These statistics describe the whole Columbus metro, not Oxford’s buildings.

  4. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Columbus Economy at a Glance, August 2026
